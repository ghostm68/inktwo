HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION • PURE MEMOIR VOICE • 40–60 MIN
LEBEN MIT EINEM KRIEGSVERBRECHER
LIFE WITH A WAR CRIMINAL
Dramatized from the 1976 Memoirs of Lina Heydrich
Expanded Focus on Her Perspective & Their Private World
BASED ON THE MEMOIR BY: Lina Heydrich
Screenplay Adaptation — Pure Narrative Voice

A tightly structured dramatization of the woman without whom
the book would not exist, and the man she refused to renounce.
NOTE ON APPROACH:
This version stays inside Lina’s recollection and the private life she shared with Reinhard. Public caricatures — the high voice, the “betrayed comrades,” the cold ego — appear only as they reached her ears or as she observed them. The jammed Sten and the grenade remain. Runtime target: 40–60 minutes.
FADE IN:
EXT. CASTLE JUNGFERN-BRESCHAN - COURTYARD - MORNING (MAY 27, 1942)

Cold spring light on the baroque facade. An open dark-green MERCEDES 320-B idles on the gravel.

REINHARD HEYDRICH (38) stands by the door — tall, blade-thin, immaculate General’s tunic, unclasped briefcase under one arm. His voice, when he speaks, is higher than most expect from the face.

LINA HEYDRICH (30), heavily pregnant, wraps a wool shawl tighter. One final embrace. She holds on a second longer than he does.

LINA
Two days in Berlin. No longer.
HEYDRICH
Two days. Then straight back to you and the children.

He climbs into the open passenger seat beside SS-Oberscharführer KLEIN. Turns. Raises a gloved hand. Lina waves until the gravel spray settles.

Beside the portal, MARTA the maid dabs her eyes.

MARTA
(voice shaking)
Oh, gnädige Frau… what an ominous departure.
LINA
(without turning)
Marta. Superstition is for people who have nothing else to do. Go inside.
INT. CASTLE JUNGFERN-BRESCHAN - DRAWING ROOM - AFTERNOON (3:00 PM)

Lina sits sewing baby garments. Boots click. DR. HORST BÖHME (Prague SiPo Commander) stands in the doorway, helmet in hand, ash-pale.

LINA
Dr. Böhme? Is something wrong with your wife?
BÖHME
(choked)
On your husband… an assassination attempt has been carried out.

The needle freezes in her fingers. Silence.

BÖHME (CONT'D)
He lives. Bulovka Hospital in Libeň. Professors are operating. A car is waiting.
LINA (V.O.)
An assassination. The word went through me. Yet I felt completely numb. Our life had taught us to expect such surprises. Being Chief of the Gestapo was never an enviable trade in Adolf Hitler’s Reich. I thought of the children — Klaus, Heider, Silke — and the one still unborn. Now it would fall on me.
INT. BULOVKA HOSPITAL - CORRIDOR / WARD - LATE AFTERNOON

Professor Gerhardt, the German ambassador, meets her. Professor Knaus, her own doctor, is already there. She is led into the room.

Reinhard has been operated on twice. Conscious. Barely able to speak.

HEYDRICH
(whisper)
Denk an unsere Kinder… Think of our children.

She is led out on unsteady legs. Knaus gives her an injection.

LINA (V.O.)
I was allowed to bring him bouillon I cooked myself. Every day he seemed better. Until the night of the third to the fourth of June.
INT. CASTLE JUNGFERN-BRESCHAN - BEDROOM - NIGHT (JUNE 3–4)

She is torn from sleep. Cold. Teeth chattering. Hands shaking. Her bag falls; she leaves it. Nothing matters.

INT. BULOVKA HOSPITAL - WARD - DAWN

He is already in a coma. They pull her away. Sedative. When she wakes — he is gone.

RADIO ANNOUNCER (V.O.)
The Obergruppenführer and General of Police, Chief of the Security Police and SD, Acting Reichsprotektor of Bohemia and Moravia, Reinhard Heydrich, has today succumbed to his injuries.

Lina sits alone in the hospital corridor, hands folded over the unborn child.

LINA (V.O.)
Fifteen years earlier I met the man who became my fate. Everything that followed — the power, the fear, the ridicule, the silence — began with a ball and a quiet proposal in a wine cellar.
DISSOLVE TO:
INT. KIEL - HENRIETTENHAUS - ROOM 18 - DAY (DECEMBER 1930)

Four young women, bored, broke, cake and malt coffee gone. LINA (19), sharp-eyed, restless.

GIRL
Lina, tell us about your aunts again.

She does the usual caricatures, but her heart is not in it. A ball is coming. A boy from the neighboring village has cancelled. She almost stays home. The others make her keep her word.

INT. TONHALLE KIEL - BALLROOM - NIGHT (DECEMBER 6, 1930)

Mostly girls. Few dancers. Lina and two friends sit in the background, ready to leave. Then two naval officers cut through the crowd. One dark and aristocratic (VON MANSTEIN). The other extraordinarily tall, blond, icy eyes, parted hair.

HEYDRICH
(clicking heels, formal bow — voice clear, slightly high)
Oberleutnant zur See Reinhard Heydrich. May I have this dance?

They dance. Later he walks her to the door of the Henriettenhaus and asks to see her again.

EXT. HOHENZOLLERNPARK - DAY (TWO DAYS LATER)

They walk the length and breadth of the park. He talks of his mother, his childhood in Halle, the conservatory, the lost family fortune. No flirtation. No performance. Only a quiet, intense desire to be known.

LINA (V.O.)
There was nothing of the lover trying to please. He gave himself as comrade, as friend — and yet it was already more. When I came home I was no longer the same person.
INT. SMALL WINE CELLAR - BRUNSWICK STREET - NIGHT

After the theatre (she cannot remember the play). They sit in near silence. She feels ordinary beside him. Then, calmly:

HEYDRICH
Will you be my wife?

Silence. Her mind races: schoolgirl, poor village teacher’s daughter, his navy regulations, her parents’ expectations.

LINA
My God, Herr Heydrich — you don’t even know my parents. You know nothing of my father’s profession. You are a naval officer. There are rules.
HEYDRICH
(smiling, quiet)
I do not want to marry your father. I want to marry you.

She tells him everything. Then she says yes.

INT. LÜTJENBRODE SCHOOLHOUSE - CHRISTMAS 1930

Red brick village school. Her father, the self-assured Dorfschulmeister, and Reinhard size each other up. Her father talks of Fehmarn seafarers, gold-rush grandfathers, inflation, the Church as worldly power. Reinhard listens with the same intensity he later brings to maps and card indexes.

FATHER
(to Lina later)
He fits our landscape.
LINA (V.O.)
I did not yet know that the navy had its own landscape — and that it was about to reject him.
INT. KIEL - NAVAL OFFICERS’ QUARTERS / LATER, A ROOM IN THE HENRIETTENHAUS

Reinhard pale, quiet. He has been summoned before a court of honor. A girl from a colonial women’s school. A sailing regatta. A promise of marriage he did not keep. Comrades who pressed the case. The chairman of the court is one of his own.

HEYDRICH
In the navy they have a pigtail for everything. Other cases were worse. One comrade’s official fiancée stayed with him when he was ill. He was finished too.

Lina presses him. She never fully believes the version that nothing physical happened. The outcome is clear: forced resignation. Two years’ transitional pay. Career ended.

LINA (V.O.)
If he had not left the navy in 1931, only God knows what would have become of us. The same men who later called him cold and ambitious were the ones who first closed the door.
INT. HALLE - HEYDRICH FAMILY APARTMENT - 1931

His mother’s face collapses when she hears. She had pinned her last hopes on this son. The house still shows traces of former wealth — porcelain, silver — but the radio has ruined the music business and the inflation has finished the rest. Bruno Heydrich, the former Heldentenor, is a large, theatrical man who still lives in the center of every room. Elisabeth is small, precise, cultured, and holds the household together with a pencil and a daily shopping list.

LINA (V.O.)
They said later he had a high voice, that he was vain, that comrades betrayed him because he was unbearable. I saw a man whose family had already fallen once, and who was falling again.
EXT. WALDTRUDERING NEAR MUNICH - DUSK (JUNE 1931)

Muddy lane. Ramshackle house. Chickens. Baron von Eberstein escorts Reinhard — now in a threadbare civilian suit — up the steps.

INT. HIMMLER BEDROOM - CONTINUOUS

HEINRICH HIMMLER (30) sits propped up in bed, wire spectacles, feverish. Nightshirt.

HIMMLER
The Baron speaks highly of your naval service. But we don’t need sailors. We need counterintelligence. The Party is full of police spies and communists.
HEYDRICH
In the Reichsmarine I specialized in radio and secure communications.
HIMMLER
(sliding pen and blank notepad across the blanket)
Thirty minutes. Sketch me an independent intelligence service for the SS.
INT. PARLOR - 25 MINUTES LATER

Reinhard’s hand moves with mechanical precision: hierarchical branches, card indexes, courier lines, counter-surveillance cells.

INT. BEDROOM - MOMENTS LATER

Himmler studies the sheet. Eyebrows rise behind the round lenses.

HIMMLER
One hundred and eighty marks a month. You report to me. Can you move to Munich at once?
HEYDRICH
Yes, Reichsführer.
LINA (V.O.)
I saw it as a young unemployed officer taking technical work. He saw a structure that could be built. Later people said the sketch itself was an act of pure ambition. He never spoke of it that way to me. He spoke of order.
INT. MUNICH - NYMPHENBURG HOUSE - NIGHT (1932)

Illegal period. SA and SS banned. The house is arranged for sudden visitors: compromising papers can vanish in seconds. The dog is the early-warning system. Lina’s bed is a kind of fortress. She heats the coke stoves every morning before dawn. No help can be kept — too dangerous, and no money.

LINA (V.O.)
I was cook, cleaner, lookout, and alarm. When Röhm and Himmler came to inspect, I had swapped the ordinary matches for exploding ones as a joke on the men who kept stealing them. The bang nearly caused a diplomatic incident. Röhm laughed until he cried. A year later he stood godfather to our first son.
INT. BERLIN - SCHLACHTENSEE HOUSE - MUSIC ROOM - EVENING (1938)

Fire burning. Reinhard and ERIKA CANARIS play a Bach double concerto with razor precision. ADMIRAL WILHELM CANARIS sips Mosel beside Lina.

CANARIS
(wry)
If only the Abwehr and the SD were as finely tuned as those two violins.
HEYDRICH
(lowering the bow, unsmiling)
Harmony requires discipline. When one instrument plays out of turn, it must be retuned. Or replaced.
LINA (V.O.)
People later said he had no friends. That is true. He believed his work forbade them. After the navy court and after Röhm, he asked Himmler never to offer the familiar “Du.” He was afraid that friendship would blunt the force of his objections. “You are my best friend,” he told me once. “We do not need to share with anyone.”
INT. SAME HOUSE - MORNING ROUTINE

Reinhard rises early. A barber comes every morning; Lina dislikes him. Newspaper during the shave. Breakfast together. Precisely at eight he is at the office. First stop: the fencing hall. Daily bouts. He had been German team champion; after that it was only maintenance of the body.

LINA (V.O.)
They mocked the high voice. They said he was an upstart who had been thrown out of the navy for chasing skirts and then invented a secret police to avenge himself. I lived with the man who practiced Bach in the evenings, who flew aerobatics over Fehmarn to land on a meadow and eat fried potatoes and Krum und Milch, who could not bear to hear the Toselli Serenade because the petty officers had forced him to play it drunk when he was a young officer. Everything he loved and everything he hated he put into that violin.
INT. CASTLE JUNGFERN-BRESCHAN - STUDY - NIGHT (JANUARY 1942)

Large map of Russia unrolled. Reinhard traces the Urals and Siberia with a red pencil. Lina enters with tea.

LINA
You are barely home and already buried in papers. What is this conference in Wannsee you have summoned everyone to?
HEYDRICH
(clinical, detached)
The final solution of the European Jewish question. We are organizing the complete transfer to the East.
LINA
To Siberia? Millions of people? How could they survive in those barrens?
HEYDRICH
Siberia has coal, ore, timber. The strong will build the roads. Naturally a great percentage will fall away through natural attrition. The rest will work. It is an immense engineering problem, Lina. Nothing more.
LINA (V.O.)
That is what he told me. I believed him. Later a friend who had seen the shooting squads wrote that the resettlement had become something else. By then Reinhard was already dead.
EXT. PRAGUE-HOLEŠOVICE - TRAM LOOP CURVE - 10:35 AM (MAY 27, 1942)

Hairpin bend. Streetcar groans. JAN KUBIŠ leans on the railing, hand inside raincoat on an anti-tank grenade. Opposite: JOSEF GABČÍK, trench coat over a folded STEN.

A pocket mirror flashes twice. Signal.

The open green Mercedes descends. Klein slows for the tight turn.

Gabčík steps into the street. Raises the Sten. Aims at Heydrich. Pulls the trigger.

CLICK. Misfire. Bolt jammed.

KLEIN
(shouting)
Herr Obergruppenführer!

Instead of ordering Klein to accelerate, Heydrich RISES in the open car. Face white with fury. He draws his 7.65 mm pistol and aims point-blank at Gabčík.

HEYDRICH
Shoot the dog!

Behind the car Kubiš steps forward and hurls the grenade underhand.

KABOOM. Rear wheel lifts. Smoke, metal, horsehair, dust.

Heydrich staggers out through the passenger door, pistol firing blindly into the fumes, then collapses face-down against the warm radiator grille.

LINA (V.O.)
They said later it was ego that made him stand up instead of fleeing. I know only that the man I married did not know how to duck.
INT. BULOVKA HOSPITAL - WARD - NIGHT (JUNE 3, 1942)

Lina beside the high iron bed. Reinhard’s face sunken, feverish. The grenade splinter has turned septic.

HEYDRICH
(barely audible)
Lina… go back to Fehmarn. Take the children home.
LINA
Hush. You will come home with us.

His eyes roll upward. Dry lips move, quoting his father’s opera:

HEYDRICH
(faint)
Yes, the world is a barrel organ… that the good Lord turns himself… and everyone must dance to the tune on the drum…

Monitors flatline. A doctor pulls her back by the shoulders.

EXT. INVALIDENFRIEDHOF BERLIN / MOSAIC HALL REICH CHANCELLERY - DAY

State funeral. Wagner’s Götterdämmerung. Hitler and Himmler. The two older boys stand in for their mother. A simple soldier’s grave with a wooden cross and his steel helmet.

LINA (V.O.)
He had always felt himself a soldier. That is how he died. Ten days later I turned thirty-two. A young widow, “rich in children,” as they used to say. My father had said at my birth: “She came into the world without help; she will go through the world without help.” I remembered that in the autumn of 1945 when I stood half-naked and penniless at the door of my parents’ house on Fehmarn, the wife of a war criminal.
INT. CASTLE JUNGFERN-BRESCHAN - JULY 1942

Daughter MARTE is born in the night of 22–23 July. Lina holds a fatherless child. Himmler appoints himself guardian. Hitler offers the castle. Complicated papers. She refuses to accept conditions that would forbid her remarriage. She stays as a kind of land-office employee so the children can remain.

EXT. GERMANY IN COLLAPSE - SPRING–AUTUMN 1945

Flight from Prague. Czech staff help pack the car. One of them, Resi, says at parting: “Don’t cry, Excellency. We will see each other again.” Years later Lina learns she was killed by her own people as a collaborator.

Forged papers. Black market. Children smuggled north in a hollow artificial leg filled with money and cigars. Checkpoints. “Please, I have no time.” Salad and cucumber in a net to look like a housewife on an errand. Eighteen checkpoints passed.

EXT. FEHMARN - PARENTS’ HOUSE - AUTUMN 1945

She knocks. Half-naked, three children, nothing left. The wife of a Kriegsverbrecher.

LINA (V.O.)
Old friends crossed the street. Authorities told me I had no claim to a widow’s pension because I had been a beneficiary of the regime. All that could be proved was that I was Reinhard Heydrich’s widow. Nothing more.
INT. BERLIN TRIBUNAL ROOM - DAY (CIRCA 1960)

Cold institutional chamber. Flashbulbs. Eight hundred schoolboys in the gallery for political education. LINA (now 49), austere in black, sits with her lawyer and her youngest daughter MARTE (18).

JUDGE
…the deceased Reinhard Heydrich was not merely an official obeying directives but an initiator of systematic mass murder. The estate remains confiscated. The petitioner has no claim to a general military widow’s pension.
LINA
(whispering to Marte, voice steel)
Look at them. In 1941 the whole country threw flowers under his wheels. Now they bring schoolboys to watch the widow of the monster pay for their sins.
MARTE
Was he what they say he was, Mother?

Lina’s hand touches the pearls at her neck. A long pause.

LINA
He was a soldier who did his duty to the end. The rest was the storm.
FADE OUT.
LINA HEYDRICH RETURNED TO THE BALTIC ISLAND OF FEHMARN.
SHE OPERATED A SMALL INN UNTIL HER DEATH IN 1985.
SHE MAINTAINED HER HUSBAND’S INNOCENCE TO THE END.
WITHOUT HER THERE WOULD BE NO BOOK.
