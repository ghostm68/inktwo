HISTORICAL FEATURE • STREAM OF CONSCIOUSNESS • 60 MIN
LEBEN MIT EINEM KRIEGSVERBRECHER
LIFE WITH A WAR CRIMINAL
Dramatized from the Recollections of Lina Heydrich
Musical Score Anchored on J.S. Bach
SCREENPLAY ADAPTATION
Incorporating the Historical Screening of Jiří Sequens’s Atentát (1964/65)

A portrait of selective memory, maternal terror, and the relics of power.
DOCUMENTARY CONTEXT:
All musical cues are drawn from Johann Sebastian Bach (Sonatas & Partitas for Solo Violin, and the Double Violin Concerto in D Minor, BWV 1043). The narrative framing begins inside an authentic 1965 Berlin screening of the Czechoslovak film Atentát. Reinhard Heydrich is ambushed in Prague-Libeň on 27 May 1942 and dies at Bulovka Hospital at 4:30 a.m. on 4 June 1942—the eighth day.

FADE IN:
INT. CZECHOSLOVAK CULTURAL CENTER, EAST BERLIN - AUDITORIUM - NIGHT (AUTUMN 1965)

[SCORE: Silence, save for the rhythmic, low CHATTER of a 35mm projector.]

A stark cone of projector light cuts through cigarette haze toward a canvas screen. The house is half full. Coats stay on.

ON SCREEN (IN 35MM BLACK-AND-WHITE): Jiří Sequens’s Atentát (1964). A low camera tracks an open dark Mercedes-Benz slowing around the hairpin curve in Prague-Libeň. Two men in trench coats step from the curb.

IN THE AUDITORIUM: Sitting isolated in row six is LINA HEYDRICH (54). Severe wool coat, hair drawn back into a plain knot. The flickering silver light sharpens her jawline. A paper program lies unopened on her lap.

ON SCREEN: The paratrooper raises a Sten gun. Pulls the trigger. CLICK. The bolt jams.

IN THE AUDITORIUM: Lina does not flinch. Her gloved fingers rise to her throat. She touches a strand of dull FRESHWATER PEARLS resting above her collar, rolling the center pearl against her collarbone—once, twice, an ancient reflex.

ON SCREEN: The second paratrooper hurls an anti-tank grenade. It shatters against the rear wheel housing. A DEAFENING BLAST.

[SCORE: The explosion cuts instantly into a single, high, vibrating violin tone: J.S. BACH — PARTITA NO. 2 IN D MINOR, BWV 1004 (CHACONNE). Solo violin, austere and piercing.]

DISSOLVE TO:
EXT. CASTLE JUNGFERN-BRESCHAN - COURTYARD - MORNING (MAY 27, 1942)

DAY ONE — THE MORNING HE LEAVES.

Blinding May sunlight. High lilacs in violet bloom, already dropping petals onto the gravel. An open dark-green Mercedes 320 idles on the stones.

LINA (30), heavily pregnant, stands on the terrace. Her apron bears faint traces of garden loam. Beside her, KLAUS (8) and HEIDER (7) chase each other across the grass.

Lina catches Klaus by the wrist, gently wiping a streak of soil from his temple with her hem.

LINA
Stay clear of the main gate, Klauschen. The trucks have blind corners.
KLAUS
Father promised to show me the black mare when he returns!

REINHARD HEYDRICH (38) steps from the double terrace doors. Field-grey tunic, cap tucked under his arm. His long, pale hand rests over Lina’s shoulder. His fingers lightly brush the pearls at her neck.

HEYDRICH
Two days with the Führer in Berlin. Then straight home. Tell the groom to keep the mare in. Klaus pulls her mouth.
LINA
(hand lingering on his cuff)
Take the closed staff car, Reinhard. Just this once. The open car is a parade.
HEYDRICH
(a quiet, thin smile)
My Czechs know their master is not afraid. A roof invites ambush. The wind keeps the head clear.

He turns, touches Klaus’s blond hair, and climbs into the front seat beside his driver, SS-Oberscharführer JOHANNES KLEIN. Not the back. Never the back. The Mercedes turns down the gravel drive.

EXT. PRAGUE-LIBEŇ - JUNCTION OF KIRCHMAYEROVA AND V HOLEŠOVIČKÁCH - DAY (MAY 27, 1942 - 10:35 AM)

DAY ONE — 10:35 A.M. THE CURVE.

Dazzling midday glare. A sharp, downhill cobblestone curve where Kirchmayerova Avenue sweeps into V Holešovičkách. Overhead, electrical tram wires crisscross the sky.

The dark-green open Mercedes-Benz 320 brakes hard to navigate the steep hairpin turn.

Standing on the pavement curb: JOZEF GABČÍK (30). He drops his raincoat to the dirt, stepping straight into the roadway directly in front of the decelerating radiator grille.

In his hands: a British STEN MK II SUBMACHINE GUN. Gabčík braces the skeleton stock against his hip, takes aim point-blank at Heydrich's chest, and squeezes the trigger.

A dry, hollow, metallic SNAP. The 9mm cartridge misfeeds. The bolt JAMS.

In the front seat, Klein reaches for the floor shift to accelerate—standard escape protocol.

Heydrich slaps Klein’s arm away with a violent backhand.

HEYDRICH
(screaming over the engine)
Halt! Sofort anhalten!

Klein stamps the floorboard brake. Tires SCREECH across the rails.

Instead of ducking, Heydrich leaps to his feet inside the open car. He unholsters his WALTHER PPK (7.65mm) and chambers a round, barking with furious, arrogant disdain.

Behind the car, JAN KUBIŠ lunges from behind a streetlamp. He swings an arm like a bowler, hurling a MODIFIED NO. 73 ANTI-TANK GRENADE.

The bomb strikes the right rear wheel well.

A CATACLYSMIC ROAR.

The blast punches the rear of the Mercedes off its suspension. The right side-panel disintegrates into flying metal, wheel splinters, pulverized seat padding, and coarse upholstery horsehair. Shrapnel cuts Kubiš across the brow; blood blinds his left eye.

Acrid, gray-black smoke engulfs the junction. Passing tram No. 3 jerks to a screeching halt.

Gabčík tosses the dead Sten into the gutter, spinning on his heel to sprint up Kirchmayerova hill.

Through the swirling cordite smoke, the passenger door violently KICKS open.

HEYDRICH EMERGES ON HIS FEET.

His uniform is shredded at the lower flank; his tunic is blackened by blast soot. Flooded with massive combat adrenaline, he feels no pain yet—only animal rage. With his Walther PPK extended, Heydrich SPRINTS into the intersection, pursuing Gabčík uphill.

CRACK! CRACK! CRACK!

Heydrich fires three wild rounds. Gabčík pulls a Colt pocket pistol, spinning mid-stride to snap return shots that ricochet off cobblestones.

Heydrich raises his Walther to fire a fourth round—CLICK. The pistol jams.

Fifteen paces up the hill, the adrenaline cliff drops out from underneath him. Heydrich STUMBLES. His face drains from red to a hideous, wet, chalky grey. A deep, guttural groan rips from his throat.

His left hand clamps over his lower left flank. Dark venous blood wells between his fingers. Internally, his 11th rib is shattered, his diaphragm torn, his spleen pulverized.

He staggers backward down the incline, clutching tram poles to stay upright, collapsing chest-first over the warm hood of the Mercedes.

HEYDRICH
(coughing, spray of blood hitting the radiator)
Fassen Sie den Hund! Den Kerl... get him!

Klein sprints after Gabčík into Brauner’s butcher shop. Left alone in the smoke, Heydrich slides off the hood into the filth of the gutter. He lies bleeding in an occupied capital, waiting for a baker's delivery van to haul him to Bulovka Hospital.

HISTORICAL & FORENSIC DOSSIER — BULOVKA TO LIDICE:

1. The Pursuit: Contemporary police reports, eyewitness depositions, and the official Gestapo investigation (the Pannwitz Report) confirm that Heydrich exited the vehicle and pursued Jozef Gabčík uphill, firing until his weapon jammed and internal hemorrhage caused immediate circulatory collapse.

2. The Eight Days: Wounded 27 May 1942. Operated on at Bulovka Hospital by Dr. Josef Hohlbaum and Prof. Dr. Walter Dick, later attended by Himmler’s physician Karl Gebhardt. A treacherous temporary rally gave way to sudden collapse on 3 June; Heydrich died at 4:30 a.m. on 4 June from severe sepsis, diaphragmatic infection, and delayed internal hemorrhage.

3. Totalitarian Reprisal (Lidice, June 10, 1942): Acting under Hitler’s direct order to “wade through blood,” the SS surrounded the village of Lidice on June 9. On June 10, all 173 men and boys over fifteen were executed; 184 women were deported to Ravensbrück; 88 children were murdered in gas vans at Chełmno. The village was leveled to the bare earth.
LINA (V.O.)
Before the empire, before the black coats and the dossiers, there was only a cashiered sailor sitting in his mother’s drafty parlor with his pride cut to ribbons.
DISSOLVE TO:
INT. HEYDRICH FAMILY APARTMENT, HALLE - NIGHT (APRIL 1931)

[SCORE: A solitary upright piano plays J.S. BACH — SARABANDE FROM PARTITA IN B MINOR, haltingly, from down the hall.]

Rain beats against tall windowpanes. Pale patches mark the wallpaper where family portraits were sold during the inflation.

REINHARD (27) sits at the table in an ill-fitting civilian suit, his naval buttons sheared off. LINA (19), sharp-eyed, pours boiling water into a stone teapot.

HEYDRICH
Discharged for dishonor. Ten years of service erased because an admiral’s daughter took offense. My father cannot walk past the conservatory without neighbors whispering.
LINA
Your father lives in thirty years ago. We live now. Look at me, Reinhard.

She takes his cold hands. Heydrich pulls a scuffed leather case from his vest. Snaps it open to reveal a delicate strand of freshwater pearls.

HEYDRICH
My mother’s father gave them to her when she sang at the Dresden Opera. She hid these in the piano case when the school failed. They stripped my rank... I swear to you, Lina, they will never take anything from us again.

He fastens the clasp behind her neck. The cold pearls settle firmly against her skin.

LINA
(touching the clasp)
Then go to Munich. Let this man Himmler see what you can build.
LINA HEYDRICH — LEBEN MIT EINEM KRIEGSVERBRECHER (1976):
“Als Reinhard im April 1931 aus der Reichsmarine entlassen wurde, brach für ihn eine Welt zusammen. Er saß tagelang im Zimmer seiner Eltern in Halle, den Kopf in die Hände gestützt, und sah keine Zukunft mehr. Ich war es, die sagte: ‚Wir fangen von vorne an. Geh nach München.‘”

[Translation: When Reinhard was dismissed from the Reichsmarine in April 1931, his world collapsed. He sat for days in his parents’ room in Halle, head in his hands, seeing no future. It was I who said: ‘We start anew. Go to Munich.’]
INT. APARTMENT ON TÜRKENSTRASSE, MUNICH - BEDROOM - NIGHT (JUNE 17, 1933)

[SCORE: Silence, save for the rhythmic clatter of a late-night streetcar below.]

Sweltering June heat. A cramped fourth-floor attic room with peeling wallpaper.

LINA (22) lies on a narrow iron cot, hair drenched and plastered to her forehead. She screams through clenched teeth as a violent contraction tears through her.

Beside her on the floor, REINHARD (29) is on his knees in an undershirt and suspenders, his tie tossed over a chair. His knuckles are white, holding Lina’s hand against his jaw. He is trembling.

REINHARD
(whispering frantically, terrified)
Hold onto me, Lina. Don't look away. Look at me. Squeeze my hand. Squeeze it as hard as you can.

A local MIDWIFE in a gray apron pulls back the sheet.

MIDWIFE
Now, Frau Heydrich. With the pain. Now!

Lina rears upward with an animal groan. Her collar pulls open—the freshwater pearls glint against her sweat-slick skin.

A sharp gasp, followed by a piercing NEWBORN CRY.

The midwife cuts the cord, wrapping the infant in a rough flour sack, placing the baby—KLAUS—onto Lina’s bare chest.

Reinhard buries his face in the hollow of Lina’s shoulder. His shoulders convulse—weeping in sheer, unvarnished relief. He presses kiss after kiss into her neck, brushing the pearls.

REINHARD
A boy, Lina. A boy... Klaus. Our Klaus.
LINA (V.O.)
We had eighty marks a month from Himmler. We had two rooms and cabbage in the pot. But on that night, he was just my husband, trembling on the floor with our son's breath on his skin. I did not understand that the uniform eats the man, stitch by stitch, until there is nothing left inside.
MATCH CUT ON THE NEWBORN'S CRY TO:
INT. BERLIN-SCHLACHTENSEE - MUSIC ROOM - EVENING (DECEMBER 1938)

[SCORE: THE CRY CUTS SHARPLY INTO J.S. BACH — CONCERTO FOR TWO VIOLINS IN D MINOR, BWV 1043 (LARGO MA NON TANTO).]

FIVE YEARS LATER. Total contrast. A fire blazes in a grand brick hearth.

Heydrich, now in the black SS uniform with silver collar runes, stands shoulder-to-shoulder with ERIKA CANARIS. Their violin bows rise and descend in razor precision.

On the sofa, ADMIRAL WILHELM CANARIS sips Mosel wine from crystal. Across the Persian carpet, Lina sits with KLAUS (5) and toddler HEIDER, building a wooden railway.

The music pauses on an exquisite, suspended cadence. Heydrich lowers his instrument.

CANARIS
If our ministries could find that same resolution, Reinhard, Europe might sleep past dawn.
HEYDRICH
Dissonance is only permitted when it leads to a decisive conclusion, Admiral. Otherwise, it is simply noise.
LINA (V.O.)
He put every ounce of tenderness he had left into Bach. For the world, he saved the iron. I told myself the home was ours, and the iron was merely the wall keeping the wolves out.
INT. BULOVKA HOSPITAL, PRAGUE - 3RD FLOOR CORRIDOR - DAY (MAY 31, 1942)

DAY FOUR. THE TREACHEROUS RALLY BEGINS.

[SCORE: J.S. BACH — SOLO VIOLIN PARTITA IN D MINOR. A brittle, high-register cadence, like ice fracturing.]

PROFESSOR DR. WALTER DICK (42), chief surgeon of Bulovka, stands by the glass nursing partition, tapping a medical chart with a silver pencil.

DR. DICK (V.O.)
In trauma surgery, we learn to fear the third day more than the first. We give it a German name: Die trügerische Besserung. The Treacherous Rally. The body stages an exquisite pantomime of health. The fever drops. The cheeks flush. The patient smiles.
INT. PRIVATE CORNER ROOM - CONTINUOUS

Heydrich sits fully upright against white bolster pillows. On his bed tray: a bowl of clear meat broth, dark rye bread, and butter. A leather dispatch folder lies open.

Lina sits close beside him on the iron chair, holding his sleeve, her shoulders loose for the first time in four days.

LINA
Eat the crust, Reinhard. You need the starch for the wound to knit.
HEYDRICH
Tell Karl Hermann Frank to execute the hostages in groups of ten. Not fifty. It creates paperwork. Tell him to have the car repaired. I want the same chassis.

He reaches out, his fingers resting over Lina’s rounded stomach, feeling the unborn child.

INT. PRIVATE ROOM - DUSK (JUNE 2, 1942)

DAY SIX. FEVER. THE WOUND STILL DRAINING.

The rally begins to splinter. Shadowy light. Bandages swathe his torso; a glass drain tube seeps dark fluid under the gauze. Heydrich's cheeks are wet with fever sweat.

HEYDRICH
Denk an unsere Kinder... Take them to Fehmarn if the front collapses.
LINA
Do not speak of collapses. You are the Protector.

His eyes stare upward into the dim plaster ceiling. His fingers twitch on the bedsheet, tracing the silent ghost fingerings of a violin passage.

INT. PRIVATE ROOM - DAY (JUNE 3, 1942 - NOON)

DAY SEVEN. THE RALLY BREAKS.

Heydrich lifts a porcelain cup of coffee to his lips. Suddenly, his jaw clenches. The cup SLATTERS onto the tray, dark coffee soaking the sheets.

His eyes bulge. His hand claws at his left flank. A sound like tearing canvas rips from his throat as his blood pressure craters.

SS doctors and Dr. Dick burst through the double doors. Alarm bells RING down the corridor.

Heydrich violently arches his back, drowning in acute septic shock, his fingernails tearing the linen as he slips into a terminal coma. Lina is shoved back against the wall, clutching her pearls.

INT. PRIVATE ROOM - DAWN (JUNE 4, 1942 - 4:30 AM)

DAY EIGHT. HE DOES NOT WAKE.

The clock ticks in the hall. The breathing changes register—wet, thin, then absent. Dr. Dick looks at the clock. Heydrich is dead.

INT. MOSAIC HALL, NEW REICH CHANCELLERY, BERLIN - DAY (JUNE 9, 1942)

[SCORE: A massive organ blasts WAGNER'S FUNERAL MARCH, hollow and deafening.]

A towering cavern of red marble, polished granite, and giant swastika standards. Wreaths of blood-red carnations stacked ten feet high.

At the front of the hall, isolated before the massive bronze catafalque, stand KLAUS (8) and HEIDER (7) in tailored black velvet sailor suits. SS officers loom behind them.

Their mother is absent—two hundred miles away, confined to bed in Bohemia.

ADOLF HITLER approaches along the marble runner, his face set in theatrical lamentation. He stops directly before the motherless boys.

Hitler extends his black-gloved hands. He cups Klaus’s trembling jaw, tilting the eight-year-old’s pale face upward toward the bronze ceiling.

HITLER
Your father had an iron heart. You must be iron for the Reich.

Klaus’s chin quivers under the leather glove. Terrified, with no hand to hold, his eyes dart across the towering sea of black uniforms.

INTERCUT WITH:
INT. CASTLE JUNGFERN-BRESCHAN - BEDROOM - DAY (JUNE 9, 1942)

LINA lies alone in the darkened bedroom, heavily pregnant, pale and shivering beneath the duvet. On the nightstand, the Volksempfänger radio blares the organ chords and Hitler's hollow voice from Berlin.

Her fingers grip the sheet until the threads strain.

LINA (V.O.)
Because I was ill and wretched, approaching my confinement, my two boys took my place in Berlin. Two hundred miles away, through the radio speaker, I heard what they had done. Reinhard was no longer my husband. He was no longer the father who lifted Klaus onto the mare. He had been eaten by the State theater. They made my boys stand like soldiers before Hitler, using our dead man to feed his furnace.
LINA HEYDRICH — LEBEN MIT EINEM KRIEGSVERBRECHER (1976):
“Weil ich mich elend und krank fühlte und meiner Niederkunft entgegensing, nahmen unsere beiden Söhne Klaus und Heider an meiner Stelle an dem Staatsakt in der Reichskanzlei teil... Die Zerstörung von Lidice war Hitlers persönliche Rache. Reinhard war bereits seit sechs Tagen tot. Mit diesem furchtbaren Geschehen hatte meine Familie nichts zu tun.”

[Translation: Because I felt ill and wretched, and was approaching my confinement, our two sons Klaus and Heider took part in my place in the funeral ceremony in the Reich Chancellery... The destruction of Lidice was Hitler's personal revenge. Reinhard had already been dead for six days. My family had nothing to do with this terrible event.]
DISSOLVE TO:
EXT. CASTLE JUNGFERN-BRESCHAN - ENTRANCE GATE - LATE AFTERNOON (OCTOBER 24, 1943)

SIXTEEN MONTHS LATER. A child's bicycle lies crushed in the gravel, its front wheel bent sideways. A Czech lorry idles, radiator ticking.

Lina falls to her knees on the road. She gathers KLAUS (10) into her lap. The boy’s blond hair is soaked in crimson; his eyes are fixed on the clouds.

LINA
(a raw, broken choke)
Klaus... Klauschen... Mother is here. Look at Mother.

The Czech lorry driver stands by his fender, palms pressed to his eyes. SS sentries run forward with rifles raised.

LINA (CONT'D)
(screaming at the guards)
DON’T TOUCH HIM! LEAVE US ALONE!

[SCORE: Silence falls instantly, total and dry. The only sound is the cold Baltic wind.]

LINA (V.O.)
They said the paratroopers killed Reinhard. But Klaus... Klaus was taken by a lorry on a sunlit afternoon. And that was when I knew: no uniform, no castle, no empire could keep anything safe.
DISSOLVE TO:
EXT. FEHMARN ISLAND - BALTIC SHORE - DUSK (1960)

[SCORE: J.S. BACH — SOLO VIOLIN PARTITA NO. 2 (CHACONNE) enters again, distant, carried on crashing waves.]

Grey dunes. Sea grass whipping in the autumn gale. The weathered guesthouse Imbria Parva stands solitary against the horizon.

LINA (49) walks along the water’s edge in heavy boots and a woolen coat, rolling the freshwater pearls between her bare fingers.

LINA (V.O.)
They come from the courts with their stamps and subpoenas. They want me to spit on his memory so they can forgive themselves. But if I say he was a monster, then Klaus’s laughter was a lie. The violin was a lie. These pearls around my neck are the only proof that any of it was real. He was a soldier who held the line assigned to him. The rest... was the storm.
LINA HEYDRICH — LEBEN MIT EINEM KRIEGSVERBRECHER (1976):
“Reinhard war ein Soldat, der seine Pflicht erfüllte bis zum letzten Atemzug. Wenn die Welt heute von Monstern spricht, sage ich: Sie kannten den Menschen nicht. Was auf Fehmarn blieb, war die Wahrheit unseres Lebens — nicht die Parolen der Sieger.”

[Translation: Reinhard was a soldier who fulfilled his duty until his last breath. When the world today speaks of monsters, I say: they did not know the man. What remained on Fehmarn was the truth of our life — not the slogans of the victors.]

EXT. FRIEDRICHSTRASSE, EAST BERLIN - CONTINUOUS (1965)

Lina pushes into the cold autumn night. Wet asphalt reflects the neon lights of the divided city. A tram bell somewhere, not this street’s.

A cold drop of rain strikes Lina’s upper eyelid. She does not wipe it away.

Her pupil dilates against the grey neon mist.

INT. SALA TERRENA, WALDSTEIN PALACE, PRAGUE - NIGHT (MAY 26, 1942)

THE NIGHT BEFORE DAY ONE.

[SCORE: BRUNO HEYDRICH'S CHAMBER MUSIC — a romantic, late-19th-century violin phrase, echoey in baroque stone.]

Candlelight reflecting off baroque gilt and vaulted ceilings.

JUMP CUT:

HIGH ABOVE THE PALACE GARDEN: The black, brooding silhouette of Hradčany Castle cutting into the night fog—looming silent, bureaucratic, and ancient over the city.

JUMP CUT:

THE FRONT ROW, WALDSTEIN PALACE (MAY 26, 1942): Karl Hermann Frank, Kurt Daluege, and eighty officers in gala dress sit with folded hands.

Lina does not see them. Beside her sits Reinhard. In profile, his sharp chin is lifted toward the ceiling, listening to his father’s composition.

Beneath the edge of her dark silk program, his bare hand slides across the velvet seat. His fingers lace into hers. Warm. A slight tremor in his pulse.

Not the architect of the police state. Just a nervous musician’s son holding his wife’s hand in the dark.

Beneath her gown, the unborn child—Marte—kicks against her ribs. Reinhard doesn't turn his head, but his thumb presses firmly into Lina’s palm. A private, conspiratorial squeeze.

LINA (V.O.)
They think we were thinking of flags. We were not thinking of flags. We were thinking of an attic in Halle where an old man couldn't pay his gas bill, while his music echoed through the palace of kings. His hand was warm. His seed was moving under my ribs. For one hour, before the sun rose and the car left the gates, the world had no uniforms. It had only us.
JUMP CUT:
EXT. FRIEDRICHSTRASSE, EAST BERLIN - NIGHT (1965)

Lina blinks. The palace, the music, the warmth of his fingers vanish.

There is only the cold Baltic mist, the wet asphalt of Friedrichstraße, and the empty grey wool of her coat. She turns up her collar and walks into the drizzle, solitary and erect.

Her thumb strokes the palm of her own bare hand—remembering the squeeze that ended twenty-three years ago.

She disappears into the mist. Somewhere a projector is still running the crypt.
